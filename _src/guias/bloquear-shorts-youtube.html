Actualizado el {{LASTMOD}} · 5 min de lectura

Quieres seguir usando YouTube para tutoriales, música o tus canales de siempre, pero cada vez que entras acabas veinte minutos deslizando Shorts. Aquí tienes lo que permite YouTube, por qué no basta y cómo quitar solo los Shorts sin desinstalar nada ni rootear el móvil.

Android 8.0 o superior · sin root · prueba de Premium de 7 días

¿Se pueden desactivar los Shorts desde YouTube?

No hay un interruptor oficial para quitarlos. Lo que existe son parches:

  • «No me interesa» en la fila de Shorts de la página de inicio. La esconde una temporada, pero vuelve.
  • Pausar el historial de reproducciones. Vacía las recomendaciones de la página de inicio, pero la pestaña de Shorts sigue ahí, a un toque.
  • Recordatorios de descanso y límites de tiempo en los ajustes de YouTube. Son avisos que se cierran con un toque, justo cuando menos ganas tienes de parar.
  • Usar YouTube desde el navegador o apps modificadas. Funciona a medias, pierdes funciones y las apps modificadas suponen un riesgo para tu cuenta.

El problema de fondo es que YouTube gana más cuanto más tiempo pasas dentro. Ningún ajuste suyo va a ser un freno de verdad.

En lugar del vídeo, te aparece una pregunta sencilla: «¿Realmente quieres verlo?». Tienes tres salidas:

How to block YouTube Shorts on Android

Updated {{LASTMOD}} · 5 min read

You want YouTube for tutorials, music and the channels you actually follow, but every visit ends with twenty minutes of swiping Shorts. Here is what YouTube lets you do, why it isn't enough, and how to remove only Shorts without uninstalling anything or rooting your phone.

Android 8.0 or newer · no root · 7-day Premium trial

Can you turn off Shorts inside YouTube?

There is no official switch. What you get are workarounds:

  • “Not interested” on the Shorts shelf on the home page. It hides it for a while, then it comes back.
  • Pausing your watch history. It empties the home page recommendations, but the Shorts tab is still one tap away.
  • Break reminders and time limits in YouTube's settings. They are prompts you dismiss with one tap, right when you least feel like stopping.
  • Watching YouTube in the browser or using modded apps. It half-works, you lose features, and modded apps put your account at risk.

The underlying problem: YouTube earns more the longer you stay. None of its own settings will ever be a real brake.

Block only Shorts with 0Dopamine

0Dopamine detects when you land in Shorts — from the tab, from a Short on the home page or from a link someone sent you — and takes you out. The rest of YouTube works as usual: search, subscriptions, long videos and playlists.

  1. Install 0Dopamine from Google Play and turn on the accessibility permission it asks for. That is what lets it see which screen is open; everything is processed on your phone.
  2. Add YouTube to your apps.
  3. Turn on “Block Shorts”. Add a schedule if you like: for example, Shorts blocked Monday to Friday and allowed at weekends.

What happens when you open a Short

Instead of the video you get one simple question: “Do you really want to see this?” Three ways out:

  • Go back to where you were in YouTube.
  • Close YouTube altogether.
  • Watch just this one, then it closes: if someone sent you a specific Short, you watch it; swipe to the next one and the app closes itself. One video doesn't turn into forty.

If you'd rather not even have that option, strict mode closes YouTube the moment it detects a Short — no screen, no buttons.

It works with the official YouTube app, Premium or not. YouTube Kids is a separate app: you can block it entirely or put it on a schedule.
